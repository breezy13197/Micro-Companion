Notes for the instructor (students would not see this)
  • How each step is tagged. Every question and test carries the companion(s) it draws on, in the same bacteria2+mycoses format as the Integration bank. In the real tool, a missed step would send the student back to that companion, and the debrief links point there now.
  • How the lab bench is scored. Five tests clear every suspect except the culprit. 5 tests = 3 stars, 6 = 2, 7 or more = 1, and each wrong accusation takes away a star (minimum 1). The two "wasted" tests are a repeat blood culture (it clears no one) and the Histoplasma urine antigen (useful, but the biopsy clears Histoplasma too).
  • Facts that need your eye. (1) The lectures list voriconazole, posaconazole, and isavuconazole as the mold-active azoles, so Treat question 2 says fluconazole is not among them. The lecture implies this but does not state it outright. (2) Mycoplasma is left out of the suspect list because no lecture names a test that rules it out. It is mentioned in the Gram stain result instead. (3) The Gram stain result says "few white cells" because the patient is neutropenic. (4) The influenza and CMV tests rely on the general PCR content in Viruses I, not on a disease-specific test from Viruses III.
  • Overlap. Mycoses “Three patient consults,” Case C, also has a neutropenic patient with Aspergillus. The difference is that Case C gives students the biopsy result. Here they have to decide to order it, and rule out bacteria and viruses first.
