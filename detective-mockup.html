Pathogen Detective · Case file 01

The fever that would not break

Draws on Bacteria I, II, and IV, Viruses I and III, and Mycoses. About 15 minutes.

Notes for the instructor (students would not see this)
  • How each step is tagged. Every question and test carries the companion(s) it draws on, in the same bacteria2+mycoses format as the Integration bank. In the real tool, a missed step would send the student back to that companion, and the debrief links point there now.
  • How the lab bench works. A test result makes suspects less likely; it never removes them, and every suspect stays selectable. The student does not have to rule everyone out. Naming Aspergillus is accepted once the biopsy has been ordered, because tissue showing the organism is the evidence the diagnosis needs. Naming it earlier gets a “reasonable working diagnosis, now get tissue” message, not a penalty. A banner on the bench says treatment does not wait for the workup.
  • How it is scored. There are no stars and no penalty for ordering extra tests. The debrief shows a four-item reasoning checklist (looked for bacteria, looked for a virus, got tissue evidence, named only diagnoses the evidence supported) and labels each test the student ordered as Decisive, Supporting, or Reasonable, adds little here.
  • Points that go beyond your lectures. These came from review feedback citing outside guidance (IDSA and CDC), and I did not re-check the guideline text: (1) a negative acid-fast smear lowers suspicion for TB but does not exclude it; (2) when invasive aspergillosis is strongly suspected, antifungal treatment starts while the workup continues; (3) repeating blood cultures can be appropriate during persistent neutropenic fever. Please confirm you are comfortable teaching each.
  • Facts that need your eye. (1) The lectures list voriconazole, posaconazole, and isavuconazole as the mold-active azoles, so Treat question 2 says fluconazole is not among them. The lectures imply this but do not state it outright. (2) Mycoplasma is left out of the suspect list because no lecture names a test that rules it out; it is mentioned in the Gram stain result instead. (3) The Gram stain result says “few white cells” because the patient is neutropenic. (4) The influenza and CMV tests rely on the general PCR content in Viruses I, not on a disease-specific test from Viruses III. (5) The biopsy explanation says Candida can switch between yeast and hyphal forms, which follows the Mycoses bank, so the biopsy only lowers suspicion for it.
  • Overlap. Mycoses “Three patient consults,” Case C, also has a neutropenic patient with Aspergillus. The difference is that Case C gives students the biopsy result. Here they have to decide to order it, and weigh bacteria and viruses first.
